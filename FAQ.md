## Reselling

**Can I resell this?**

Yes. CC0 permits it, including selling it completely unchanged. We are not going to stop you
and we have no claim to make.

Be clear about what you are taking on, though. You would be selling something that is available
free, from us, to anyone who looks. Buyers do find out. When they do, the refund requests, the
chargebacks, the reviews and whatever action the marketplace takes are between you and them.

We will not step in on your behalf. We will not confirm you as an authorised seller. We do not
support your customers. And we will not take this down or stop giving it away so that your
listing looks better.

If you do sell it, the honest version is to add something real — your own work, setup, support,
a service — and charge for that. Selling the free file as-is is legal. It is also your
reputation, not ours.

**Can I put it in a paid bundle or a membership?**

Yes, and everything above still applies, whether it is one file or a hundred.

**Can I upload it to another marketplace or freebie site?**

Yes — CC0 allows redistribution, paid or free. Two things to keep in mind: please do not
present yourself as an official GetFreeEbooks channel, and most marketplaces have their own
rules about listing public domain work. That part is between you and them.

**Can I put it on GitHub, or inside my own project?**

Yes. No attribution needed and no licence file to carry across.

## What is and is not covered

**What does "brandless" mean?**

No front or back cover, no logo, no header, no footer, no watermark, no URL in the margin.
Every page is the printable itself and nothing else.

That is deliberate. A public domain file with somebody's branding across it is only nominally
free — you could not put it in your own book, sell it, or hand it to a client without stripping
the branding off first. These files have nothing to strip.

**Can I put these in a book I sell?**

Yes. That is the point of releasing them this way. No attribution, no permission, no royalty.

**Can I change them?**

However you like. They are PDFs, so a vector editor is the practical route for anything beyond
printing.

**What paper size are they?**

A4 unless the filename says otherwise. Most print acceptably on US Letter with *fit to page*,
though margins will differ slightly.

**Are the fonts included?**

Type is embedded in the PDFs as those fonts' licences allow. No font files are redistributed
separately, so you cannot extract a font from these and use it elsewhere.
